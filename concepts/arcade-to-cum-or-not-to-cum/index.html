    Arcade: To cum or not to cum

    To cum or not to cum (game type to-cum-or-not-to-cum) is a night at the fair. You watch clips from your own library, every clip you sit out pays tickets, and you spend those tickets at four booths on the midway wheel — a new channel, a spin of the mystery wheel, a lucky charm from the trinket stand, or a gamble on how the night ends.

    The game is levelless: a single public level, Main stage (slug main-stage), is seeded so sessions have something to attach to. All rules run in the browser; the backend engine only stores the latest snapshot and closes the session on the final outcome.

    The night loop

    1. Pick a difficulty — sets every price, how fast your multiplier grows, and the odds at the finish booth
    2. Pick a channel — the first pick is free and cannot be cancelled
    3. Watch — clips play full screen; watched time pays tickets into the hub of the midway wheel
    4. Spend at the midway — change channel, spin the wheel, buy a trinket, or try to finish
    5. The night ends — the finish booth decides whether you win, lose, or keep going

    Every booth, stand and reveal is a menu over the clips, and opening one pauses every screen; the clips pick up where they left off when you close it.

    Difficulties

    Picked once, before the night starts, and shown in the top rail for the rest of the run.

    Good girl is the default. Harder modes also get worse odds at the finish booth (see below). The trinket column is the factor applied to every charm's list price, rounded to a tenner.

    Earning tickets

    • Watched time is worth 1 ticket per 5 seconds (per 4 or 3 with the pocket watch), plus a flat 1 ticket for reaching the end of the clip (2 or 3 with the ticket punch).
    • Tickets are only handed over when a clip is watched out. A clip you skip halfway pays nothing.
    • The last stretch of watched time and the completion bonus are handed over as one payout, so the number printed on the stub is exactly what lands on the counter.
    • Long clips pay along the way, in blocks of 30 watched seconds — but a block is only split off when more than 10 seconds are still to come, so a short tail always rides along on the final payout.
    • Only forward playback counts. Scrubbing, pausing or looping back never mints tickets.
    • Every payout is multiplied by your current multiplier (and by the golden-clip factor), then flies from the screen that won it to the counter as a ticket stub.

    Golden clips

    Each clip has a 5% chance of being golden (12% once you own the rabbit's foot). A golden clip is dressed with a gold frame, corner glints and a "Golden clip — pays ×5 · watch it out" banner, and pays ×5 for as long as it runs.

    The multiplier

    You start at ×1. The only way it grows is by accepting a challenge from the mystery wheel: the first accepted challenge adds your difficulty's first step, every one after that adds the smaller step. It never goes back down — not even when the finish wheel burns your tickets. Denying a challenge changes nothing; the tickets you paid for the spin are already spent.

    The mystery wheel

    Buying a spin plays a wheel animation, then presents a challenge card with Accept and Deny. Per bought spin you get one re-spin at half price (rounded up, and free with the lucky horseshoe), before you decide.

    The wheel remembers what it already gave you: a challenge that has come up is held back until every other challenge in the pool has come up once, and only then does the cycle start over. So no repeats back to back, and a re-spin can never hand you the same card again.

    Every challenge in the pool has the same chance of coming up. The only rule that narrows the pool is dress/undress: a dress challenge can only appear when you are not already dressed by an earlier challenge, an undress challenge only when you are.

    Product challenges need a stocked collection

    Challenges that draw from products resolve to a real item from your collection, matched by category (sub-categories count too), and show its photo and name on the card. If the category is empty, the challenge still comes up — it just falls back to generic wording ("a toy from your collection", "your highest heels"). Fill in your products to get concrete instructions.

    Outfit templates

    Doll up picks one pre-composed look and dresses it slot by slot, so the pieces always go together. The ten templates are schoolgirl, pole dancer, office lady, night out, boudoir, beach day, latex doll, casual day, bedtime and showgirl. Each slot (wig, lipstick, bra, heels, …) is filled with a real, non-repeated item from your collection where one exists; slots your collection cannot fill are left to you.

    The booths

    There is no bar along the bottom any more. The whole midway is one wheel in the bottom-right corner: a brass rim of chasing bulbs, a wedge per booth, and your ticket count in the hub. A wedge you can afford lights up; the moment one opens the rim gives a tick, so a booth announces itself by moving rather than by hanging a sign over the clip.

    Clicking the wheel spins it and throws the midway open across the screen — four lit booths side by side under a striped awning, each dropping in on its own beat. Pick one and it opens; the wheel closes behind you.

    Booth What it does
    Channel Re-opens the channel booth and switches the program
    Wheel Buys one spin of the mystery wheel
    Trinkets Opens the trinket stand — lucky charms that keep working
    Finish Gambles everything on the verdict — to cum, or not to cum

    Walking up to the trinket stand is free; it charges per charm instead. Its wedge quotes the cheapest thing you could still buy there, and reads "Sold out" once every charm is yours.

    The wheel is shuttered while an overlay is open, while paused, and during the victory lap.

    Channels

    Channels are your theater presets: a name, a screen count and one content filter per screen (tags, actors, and everything else a theater filter holds). Up to 4 screens play at once; presets with more are clamped. Only the first screen carries sound.

    There is always a free-for-all option, The whole fair — one screen, unfiltered.

    A dark channel is free to leave

    If every screen in the picked channel comes up empty, the booth re-opens at no cost and without a cancel button. Picking a channel also resets each screen's playhead, since a new channel is a new feed.

    The trinket stand

    Everything else at the fair is spent the moment you buy it. A trinket keeps working for the rest of the night. Most are bought once; two have a second rung you can come back for.

    Trinket Price (Good girl) What it does
    Brass ticket punch 350 · 700 Watching a clip out pays 2 instead of 1, and 3 at the second rung
    Rabbit's foot 300 Golden clips go from 5% to 12%
    Lucky horseshoe 400 The wheel's re-spin is free instead of half price
    Bingo card 450 25 tags that tick themselves as you watch
    Insurance slip 500 The next Denied burns half your tickets instead of all of them — then the slip burns too

    Prices scale with the difficulty (see the difficulty table). A charm you already own shows the rung you are on and what the next one would do; the insurance slip is the only one that is used up, and once it has saved you the stand will sell you another.

    Trying to finish

    Buying a finish spends the tickets up front and reveals one weighted verdict. With the spyglass an odds sheet comes first, and you can still walk away without paying. The weights depend on the difficulty (they add up to 100, so read them as percentages).

    The sissygasm verdict shows a countdown with an I did it button. Pressing it wins the night; letting the clock run out is a game over. The deadline is stored as a timestamp, so refreshing does not buy you extra time.

    Cooldown and the end screen

    A won night lands in the cooldown phase: the screens keep playing for as long as you want, the ticket counter is frozen, and a slim banner offers Leave the fair. Leaving shows the end screen, which comes in two flavours — completed (gold) and game over (dark). Both list tickets earned, clips watched, challenges accepted and your final multiplier, with Play again and Back to the arcade.

    Resuming a night

    Every change to the run is written to browser storage, including the difficulty, tickets, multiplier, channel, the playhead of each screen, the charms you bought, the challenges the wheel already used and any running sissygasm deadline. Close the tab or refresh and the front gate offers Resume your night instead of Step right up; the screens pick up on the clip they were on rather than replaying from the top. Finished nights are cleared, so they always start over, and New game on the menu discards a saved run — and its tickets — on the spot.

    Controls

    Input Action
    Space (configurable) Pause / resume the clips
    ESC Open / close the quit dialog
    Arrow down / arrow up Next / previous clip (an unfinished clip pays nothing)
    Arrow right / arrow left Scrub 5 seconds forward / back inside the running clip
    Scroll or swipe up/down Reel through the clips on that screen

    The top rail mirrors the transport buttons and adds mute, panic mode and leaving the fair.

    Hiding a clip

    Every screen carries a hide button in its bottom-right corner, the same one theater has and doing the same thing: the clip is hidden from your library and the reel moves straight on. Hiding a video hides its fragments with it, exactly as in theater. You were never going to watch it out, so it pays nothing.

    The bingo card

    Bought at the trinket stand, the card hangs along the bottom edge of the screen for the rest of the night: cream stock, a red BINGO banner across the head, and 25 tags drawn from the ones you use most.

    Every clip that plays daubs whatever tags it matches — you never click anything. A completed row or column pays, the full card pays a lot more, and each line pays only once. Fold the card away with the chevron in the banner when you would rather see the clip.

    Settings

    Two options, saved as user settings on the backend:

    Setting Description
    Sound on by default How every new night starts. You can still mute in-game
    Pause key The key that pauses and resumes the clips. Default Space; press the button and hit a key to rebind, Escape cancels

    Content filtering lives in the channel

    There are no tag or actor filters on the settings screen — what you see is decided by the channel you pick, which is a theater preset with its own filters per screen. Build the presets you want in theater and they show up in the channel booth.
